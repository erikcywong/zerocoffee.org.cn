What is verifiable today

Three things decide whether a footprint is evidence or decoration: the boundary you draw, how much of it is metered rather than estimated, and whether the baseline was co-signed before the improvement happened. Everything below follows from those three.

Line itemTypical shareWhere the number comes from
Electricity61.5%Hourly meter × marginal grid factor for that hour
Embedded hardware17.9%Capital cost amortised over 3 years, allocated per plantlet
Outbound transport10.3%Distance × mode × load factor, not a flat average
Packaging6.2%Material mass × published factor
Nutrients4.2%Mass applied × production factor
The shares above come from one reference aeroponic nursery. They are not a default: a soil-grown farm will show a very different split, which is exactly why the boundary has to be declared per site rather than borrowed from a template.

The boundary we use

Cradle to gate, with the functional unit fixed at one delivered plantlet. Gate is chosen over farm-gate because the buyer's claim starts when the tray arrives, and transport is usually 10% of the total — small enough to be honest, large enough to matter.

Capital equipment is the contested part. A carbon box, a pump and a sensor set are bought once and used for years, so their carbon is amortised over the asset life and allocated to every plantlet that passes through. We publish the amortisation period because changing it from three years to five moves the result by about four percentage points.

Evidence requirements by grade

GradeMetered shareSamplingWhat you must supply
A≥ 70%15-minute intervalsCalibrated meter certificates, 12 months of readings
B≥ 45%Hourly intervalsMeter readings plus purchase invoices for the gaps
Cbelow 45%Daily or invoice-basedInvoices only — the result is indicative, not bankable

The grade is not a quality opinion. It is arithmetic: the share of the total that traceably came from a measured quantity. A grade C label still has value internally; it just cannot carry a buyer-facing reduction claim.

What we will refuse

  • A reduction claim without a co-signed baseline. If the baseline was estimated after the fact, there is nothing to reduce against.
  • A footprint built on a single annual electricity invoice. The spread across a day is 87%; one number cannot represent it.
  • A label for a lot we cannot identify. If the batch cannot be tied to specific trays, the label belongs to nobody.

Worked example — 240,000 plantlets a year

A nursery producing 240,000 plantlets annually in 480-plantlet batches. At 0.195 kgCO₂e per delivered plantlet the annual footprint is about 46.8 tonnes; the batch total is 93.6 kg. That farm issues 500 labels a year, each carrying its own hash and registry entry.

Common questions

Can a farm verify without metering?

Yes, but the result is a grade C review of invoices, not a verified footprint. The label wording changes accordingly and we say so on the record.

How much of the footprint is actually under the grower's control?

Electricity is 61.5% of the total and the daily spread in grid intensity is 87%, so scheduling the fog pump moves the number more than buying a better pump.

Do you verify the harvest quality too?

No. We verify the carbon claim only. Coffee quality grading is a different discipline with different liabilities.
