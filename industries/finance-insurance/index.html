What a lender actually needs

Three questions decide whether a carbon claim supports a financial decision: is the number traceable to a source document, is the method stable enough to compare two borrowers, and is there somebody to hold responsible if it is wrong. A verified statement answers all three; a sustainability report usually answers none.

The assurance ladder

LevelWhat it meansTypical use
ScreeningDesk review of published dataPortfolio triage, no covenant
Agreed-upon proceduresWe check specified items and report facts onlyCovenant monitoring
Limited assuranceConclusion with a stated uncertaintyLoan pricing, ESG-linked terms
Verification statementFull re-derivation against a published methodCollateral, underwriting file

Most carbon claims marketed to financial institutions sit at level one and are priced like level three. The difference in cost is roughly a factor of six, and it is visible in the engagement letter — if there is one.

What an underwriting file contains

  • The verified footprint and the statement it came from, with the method version.
  • Data lineage: which meter, which period, which calibration certificate.
  • An uncertainty range and the sampling that justifies it.
  • A description of what would invalidate the claim (baseline drift, boundary change, meter failure).
  • The verifier's independence declaration.

What we will refuse

  • Signing an assurance opinion on data we did not test. Sampling is not optional.
  • A conclusion written by the borrower's consultant and presented for signature.
  • Confidentiality terms that prevent us from stating the basis of the conclusion. An assurance opinion whose basis is secret is not assurance.

Worked example — a 40-farm cooperative loan

A cooperative borrows against a verified low-carbon supply agreement. The lender needs to know that the label the buyer pays for will keep being issued, so the file has to cover not the last label but the process that produces the next one.

Common questions

Do you work with the borrower's auditor?

Yes — we produce the evidence, they form the financial opinion. We do not opine on creditworthiness.

Can a statement be used for green bonds?

Not by itself. Bond frameworks require additional reporting and, in most markets, an accredited verifier.

What about greenwashing liability?

The statement limits the claim to what was tested. Anything beyond that boundary is the issuer's liability, and we say so in the report.
