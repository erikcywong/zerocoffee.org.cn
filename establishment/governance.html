Impartiality and governance

Independence is a structure, not a statement of intent. These are the bodies, what each may decide, and where each is stopped.

Who may decide what

BodyPowerLimit
BoardBudget, appointments, strategyCannot touch case conclusions or working papers
Impartiality committeeCan veto any commercial decisionChair must not come from a verified industry
Technical leadMethod interpretation, dispute rulingsAppointed by the committee; board cannot dismiss
Verification teamIndependent conclusion, qualified opinion, refusalSales cannot intervene in findings

Seven controls

  1. No equity in any party we verify, declared annually by every member of staff.
  2. Fixed fees, never a share of the premium, the label revenue or a carbon price.
  3. Sales staff cannot alter findings, scope or conclusions.
  4. The technical reviewer holds a veto on issuance.
  5. No advisory work for a client we verify, and a two-year cooling-off after any prior role.
  6. Conflicts are recorded in a register that the committee reviews each quarter.
  7. Refusal is a documented outcome, not an embarrassment: the file records what was missing.

Appeals

Records

Working papers, sampling evidence and the technical review are retained for a minimum of seven years, longer where the label is still valid. A verification whose working papers no longer exist cannot be defended, and a body that cannot defend its old work is not a body.

The most common breach of independence is not corruption. It is convenience: helping a friendly client prepare the data, then verifying the help. We do not take those engagements.
