Accreditation

The difference that costs you a tender

Verification evaluates a claim. Certification evaluates you. Confusing the two is the fastest way to be excluded from a procurement process you were otherwise qualified for.

ZERO CARBON VERIFY · ESTABLISHMENT NOTE · 5 MIN READ

The words are used interchangeably in marketing material and they are not the same thing. One attaches to a number, the other attaches to an organisation.

Side by side

VerificationCertification
ObjectA specific claim or reportA management system or product
OutputStatement with a conclusionCertificate with a scope
ValidityPer engagement, per yearPer certificate cycle
StandardISO 14064-3, ISO 14067ISO/IEC 17065, ISO 9001, ISO 14001
Wrong word costsCredibilityA tender disqualification

Why procurement cares

A tender that asks for “an accredited verification” and receives “we are a certified body” gets rejected by an evaluator doing their job. The correct sentence names the standard and the accreditation: *verified in accordance with ISO 14067 by a body accredited to ISO/IEC 17065*.

Where we sit

We perform verification. We are not accredited yet, which means our statements are valid as professional opinions and not as accredited conformity assessment. Every document says that in the utility bar, not in a footnote.

If a procurement asks for accredited verification today, the honest answer is that we cannot supply it yet. Saying so early is cheaper than being found out at evaluation.

A sentence that survives evaluation

“Carbon footprint verified against ISO 14067 by an independent body
applying ISO 14064-3. Statement ZC-2026-XXXXX. Method CJV-MTH-1.0.”

That sentence is defensible because each clause can be checked: the standard exists, the statement reference resolves, and the method is published.
