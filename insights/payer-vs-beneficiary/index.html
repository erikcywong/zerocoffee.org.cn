Independence

Why the payer cannot be the beneficiary

When one entity issues the label, sells the premium and takes a share of the insurance, its numbers stop working as evidence — not because it lied, but because the structure makes the claim untestable.

ZERO CARBON VERIFY · ESTABLISHMENT NOTE · 6 MIN READ

A verification body is only worth what its independence is worth. If the party being verified can also profit from a favourable result, the conclusion is not evidence; it is sales collateral with a signature.

The three arrangements that void a claim

  1. Equity. The verifier or its people own part of the client. Every favourable conclusion enriches them.
  2. Commission. Payment tied to the outcome — a share of the premium, a bonus on issuance.
  3. Concentration. One client is such a large share of revenue that refusal would be existential.

None of these require dishonesty to do damage. They only require that an awkward finding be slightly easier to soften, and the incentive does the rest.

The consultation trap

The most common breach is not corruption, it is convenience. A client asks for help preparing the data, the verifier helps because it is friendly and billable, and then verifies its own advice. Nobody thinks of it as wrongdoing; the file looks tidy.

We do not take advisory engagements with clients we verify. If a client needs help preparing evidence, they get a different firm or they get a refusal.

Why this costs money

Independence has a price: a separated body cannot sell the software, the hardware or the implementation. It earns only on verification, which means the fee has to cover a whole organisation whose only product is judgement. That is why verified carbon costs more than a self-declared label, and why the cheaper one is cheaper.
